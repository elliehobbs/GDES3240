Isamu Noguchi (1904–1988) was a Japanese American artist and designer whose career spanned more than six decades. His work encompassed sculpture, furniture, architecture, and landscape design, earning him recognition as one of the most influential artists of the twentieth century.

For Noguchi, sculpture extended beyond the object itself. He explored how forms interact with their surroundings, using material, scale, and spatial relationships to transform the way people experience their environment.

Humpty Dumpty

Year
1946
Dimensions
59 × 20 3/4 × 17 1/2 in.
Medium
Ribbon slate

In the mid-1940s, Isamu Noguchi began producing some of his most original works, comprised of interlocking, rounded forms in materials such as wood and gray slate. Humpty Dumpty, an emblematic work from this period, was carefully constructed so that its nine parts would fit together and stay in place without glue or screws. As Noguchi explained, “everything I do has an element of engineering in it—particularly since I dislike gluing parts together or taking advantage of something that is not inherent in the material. I am leery of welding or pasting. It implies taking an unfair advantage of nature.”
